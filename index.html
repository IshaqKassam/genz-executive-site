The new executive

Executive is not a title. It is how you work.

For a long time, being an executive meant a corner office, years of waiting and a title someone else decided to give you. That picture is out of date. Today an executive is anyone who owns outcomes, decides with judgement, delivers what they promise and lifts the people around them. You can start that this year, in whatever you are doing.

The old picture
  • Wait for the title, then start acting senior
  • Power comes from rank
  • One ladder, one company, one path
  • Skills picked up slowly, by watching
The new picture
  • Start with ownership today, in the work in front of you
  • Trust comes from results, clear thinking and keeping your word
  • Many paths: a job, a business, freelancing, leading a community
  • Skills built on purpose, in short daily practice, with modern tools

You can be an executive in your own arena

In a job

Own a problem, report back and become the person the team relies on.

At university

Run a society, lead a group project or build a track record before you graduate.

Starting a business

A founder is the executive of everything: sales, customers, people and promises.

Freelancing

Clients buy your clarity and reliability as much as your skill.

Leading a community

A club, church group or volunteer team runs on the same habits.

Four habits make an executive in any of these: own the outcome, decide with judgement, deliver what you promise, and lift the people around you. That is exactly what the Gravitas Program trains.

Four quick wins

Four moves that change how you are seen.

01

Get noticed

The move: send a five-line Friday update to your manager. Visible work is trusted work.

“Done this week, next week, one thing I need.”
02

Get heard

The move: answer first, reasons second. One sentence that makes a room listen.

“My recommendation is X. Here is why.”
03

Get clear

The move: ask one question in week one that turns guesswork into a clear brief.

“What does a great first 90 days look like to you?”
04

Get direction

The move: picture the role you want next, work back to this quarter, and check in every Friday.

“What is the one thing this quarter that moves me forward?”
