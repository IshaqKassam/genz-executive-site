Work smarter with AI. Stay entirely yourself.

A practical guide to using AI at work: prompts you can copy today, how to build a second brain like Notion, simple workflows that save hours, and how to handle your files safely.

Start with the prompts →Build your second brain
Start here

Five rules. Before any prompt.

Think of AI as a fast, tireless intern. It can draft, summarise and suggest. It cannot be accountable. You are.

1

You own the answer

AI drafts, you decide. Your name goes on the work, so read every line before it leaves your hands.

2

Give it context

Say who you are, who the reader is, what you want and what good looks like. Better context beats clever wording.

3

Iterate, do not accept

Treat the first answer as a draft. Ask for a shorter, clearer or stronger version, and tell it what to change.

4

Verify what matters

AI can sound sure and be wrong. Check facts, numbers, names, laws and quotes against a reliable source.

5

Protect other people's data

Follow your employer's policy. Remove names, numbers and anything confidential before you paste.

The shape of a good prompt

Four parts. Every time.

  • Context: who you are, who the reader is, what is going on
  • Task: exactly what you want done
  • Format: length, structure and tone of the answer
  • Check: ask it to flag what it is unsure about or has assumed
Weak

Write an email to my boss about the project.

Strong

I am a junior analyst updating my manager, who is busy and prefers short messages. Write a 100-word email saying the Q4 report will be two days late because the data arrived late. Offer one fix and ask for a decision by Friday. Friendly, direct tone. Flag anything you assumed.

Practical use cases

Fifteen prompts you can use today.

Copy a prompt, replace the [brackets] and paste it into your AI tool of choice. Always read the answer critically before you use it.

Say it point-first

Rewrite the message below so the main point is in the first sentence, then the reason, then what I need from the reader. Keep it under 120 words and keep my tone polite and direct. Message: [paste your draft]

Turn notes into a weekly note

From the notes below, write a short update for my manager with three headings: Done, Next, Need from you. Use plain words and no more than two lines under each heading. Notes: [paste your notes]

Handle a tricky email

I need to reply to this email without sounding defensive. My goal is [goal]. Write three versions: warm, neutral and firm. Under each, explain in one line when to use it. Email: [paste the email]

Stress-test my plan

Here is my plan: [plan]. Act as a sceptical but fair senior colleague. List the five biggest ways this could fail, the assumption behind each, and one cheap way I can test each assumption this week.

Compare options

I am choosing between [option A] and [option B]. My priorities are [priorities]. Make a table scoring each option against my priorities, then tell me what extra information would change your recommendation.

Pre-mortem

Imagine it is six months from now and [project] has failed. Write the most likely reasons it failed, ordered by likelihood, and what I could do now to prevent each one.

Explain it at my level

Explain [topic] to me as if I am a smart graduate with no background. Use one everyday example from East African life, then give me three questions to check I understood.

Quiz me

I am learning [topic]. Ask me five questions one at a time, increasing in difficulty. After each answer tell me what I got right, what I missed and the one idea to remember.

Summarise and apply

Summarise the text below in five bullet points, then list three actions I could take at work this month because of it. Text: [paste text or upload the file]

Meeting prep

I have a meeting with [person, role] about [topic]. Give me: the three outcomes I should aim for, five smart questions to ask, and two objections I should expect with a short reply to each.

Practise a hard conversation

Play [person, role] who is [attitude, for example busy and sceptical]. I will practise asking for [what I want]. Respond realistically, one turn at a time, and after five turns give me feedback on what worked and what to change.

Interview rehearsal

Act as an interviewer for [role] at [type of company]. Ask me one question at a time, mix behavioural and technical questions, and after each answer score it out of 5 and show me a stronger version.

Spreadsheet help

I use [Excel or Google Sheets]. My data has these columns: [list]. Write the formula or steps to [goal], explain it in plain words and show me one example row.

Find patterns in data

Here is a small table of data: [paste]. Tell me three things that stand out, one thing that looks wrong or unusual, and which chart would show the story best. Do not invent numbers that are not in the data.

Brainstorm, then narrow

Give me 15 ideas for [goal]. Then group them into themes, pick the three most practical for someone with [limits such as time or budget], and explain why.

Build a second brain

One place for everything. Findable in seconds.

A second brain is a simple system for capturing what you learn, organising it lightly and finding it again when you need it. It works in Notion or any notes app. The structure follows the well-known PARA method by Tiago Forte, simplified here for a young professional.

  1. 1

    Choose one home for everything

    Pick one tool and stay with it for 30 days. Notion works well, and so do Obsidian, OneNote or Apple Notes. The tool matters less than using one place.

  2. 2

    Create five top-level pages

    Inbox (capture fast), Projects (things with an end date), Areas (ongoing responsibilities like your career or health), Library (notes from books and courses), Archive (finished work).

  3. 3

    Build three simple databases

    Notes, Projects and People. Give each a few properties only: status, date, area and a tag. Too many properties kills the habit.

  4. 4

    Capture without sorting

    Everything goes to the Inbox first: ideas, tasks, links, meeting notes. Sorting happens once a week, not in the moment.

  5. 5

    Do a ten-minute weekly sort

    Move each Inbox item to a project, an area or the Library, or delete it. Add one line on why it matters.

  6. 6

    Connect AI to your brain

    Use your tool's built-in AI if it has one, or copy a page into your AI chat. Ask questions across your notes and have AI draft summaries, plans and updates from them.

Prompts for your brain

Set up my brain

I want to build a personal knowledge system in [Notion/Obsidian/OneNote]. My role is [role] and my main goals this year are [goals]. Propose a simple structure with five top-level pages, the properties for a Projects table and a People table, and a 10-minute weekly routine. Keep it minimal.

Ask my notes

Using only the notes I paste below, answer this question: [question]. Quote the line you used for each point. If the notes do not contain the answer, say so. Notes: [paste notes]

Turn a messy page into structure

Turn the messy notes below into a clean page with: a one-line summary, key points, decisions, actions with owners, and a short list of things I should look up. Notes: [paste notes]

Workflows

Four routines that save hours.

A workflow is a small repeatable path. The same five steps, every time, so the work gets easier and better.

Meeting to actions

Never leave a meeting without knowing who does what by when.

  1. Take rough notes during the meeting, even in fragments
  2. Paste them into your AI tool right after
  3. Ask for decisions, actions, owners and dates
  4. Send the clean summary to attendees within the hour
  5. Copy the actions into your project list

Turn these meeting notes into: 1) decisions made, 2) actions with owner and date, 3) open questions. If an owner or date is missing, mark it as MISSING instead of guessing. Notes: [paste notes]

The weekly review

Ten minutes every Friday keeps your work visible and your brain light.

  1. Open your project list and last week's notes
  2. List what you finished and what slipped
  3. Ask AI to spot patterns and draft your update
  4. Choose the three priorities for next week
  5. Save the review in your second brain

Here is what I did this week and what slipped: [paste]. Draft a five-line update for my manager, name any pattern you notice in what slipped, and suggest three priorities for next week based on my goals: [goals].

Learn from anything

Turn books, articles and talks into something you actually use.

  1. Capture the source: link, title and why it matters
  2. Summarise in your own words first
  3. Ask AI to challenge or extend your summary
  4. Write one action you will try
  5. File it in your Library with a tag

Here is my summary of [source]: [paste]. Tell me what I missed, one idea I may be misreading, and one small experiment I can run at work this week to test the main idea.

Draft, critique, finish

Use AI to make your own thinking better, not to replace it.

  1. Write a rough draft yourself, even if it is messy
  2. Ask AI to critique it against a clear goal
  3. Fix the two biggest issues yourself
  4. Ask AI to check tone and typos only
  5. Read it aloud once, then send

Here is my draft: [paste]. My goal is [goal] and my reader is [reader]. Do not rewrite it. Instead give me the two biggest weaknesses, one thing that works well and any sentence that could be misunderstood.

Files and documents

Keep your files clean, safe and useful.

Name files so you can find them

Use date, topic and version: 2026-11-19_Q4-report_v2. Keep one folder per project and one Archive folder per year. Your future self will thank you.

Keep one "About me" file

A short note with your role, goals, audience and tone of voice. Paste it at the start of a chat, or save it in the tool's project or custom instructions, so you do not repeat yourself.

Keep a prompt library

Save the prompts that worked in one note with a title and a line on when to use it. Improve them each time. This becomes your personal toolkit.

Ask questions of a document

Upload or paste a report, policy or contract and ask for a summary, key dates, risks or action items. Always ask where in the document each answer came from, then check it.

Clean and convert

AI can turn messy text into a table, a table into a chart suggestion, notes into a checklist or a long document into a one-page brief. Check the result against the original.

Know what never to upload

Do not paste passwords, customer personal data, confidential contracts or anything your employer has not approved. When in doubt, remove names and numbers first.

Use it well

Smart, safe and honest.

AI should make you more capable, not more careless. Your reputation is the asset.

  • Check your workplace policy before using AI with anything about your employer or customers.
  • Never paste personal data about other people. Kenya’s Data Protection Act applies to personal information.
  • Verify facts and numbers from a trusted source. AI can invent details that sound right.
  • Be honest about help. If someone asks how you made something, say so. Never pass off AI work you cannot explain.
  • Keep learning the basics. Use AI to go faster at skills you understand, not to skip learning them.
Your first 30 days

A simple plan to make it stick.

  1. Week 1

    Start small

    Pick one tool. Use it daily for one real task: a message, a summary or a plan. Save your three best prompts.

  2. Week 2

    Build your brain

    Create the five pages and three databases. Move your current projects in and start capturing.

  3. Week 3

    Add two workflows

    Use Meeting to actions and the Weekly review every time they apply. Time how much faster you finish.

  4. Week 4

    Make it yours

    Write your About me file, tidy your prompt library and do a review: what saved time, what felt risky, what to keep.
