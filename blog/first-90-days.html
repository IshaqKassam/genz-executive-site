Trust

Your first 90 days: three moves that make people trust you

Trust is not a personality trait. It is a pattern other people notice: you do what you said, you tell them early when something is off, and you leave things clearer than you found them. You can build that pattern from your first week, with no title and no permission.

1. Write down what you were asked to do, and read it back

Most early mistakes are not about skill. They are about a task understood one way by you and another way by your manager. After any request, send two lines: what you understood, and when you will deliver it. It takes a minute and removes most surprises.

2. Deliver one small thing early

Pick something visible and useful that you can finish quickly, and finish it before the deadline. One early, solid result changes how people see everything you do after it.

Be the person who says it early.

3. Flag problems while they are small

Telling your manager on Tuesday that Friday is at risk feels uncomfortable. Telling them on Friday is worse. Bring the problem, what you have tried and what you propose. That is what a colleague people can rely on does.

Try it this week

  • Read back your next three requests in writing.
  • Choose one quick win and put a date on it.
  • Write down one thing you will flag early next time.
