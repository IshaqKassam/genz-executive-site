Relationships

Social capital: the career asset nobody teaches you

Skills get you considered. Relationships decide who gets chosen. Social capital is the trust, goodwill and reputation you build with other people, so that they want to work with you, back you and open doors you cannot see. Nobody teaches it, yet it matters just as much for an employee as for someone starting a business.

Why it matters

Opportunities, introductions, honest feedback and sponsors all travel through people. Sociologist Mark Granovetter's well-known research on how people find jobs showed that useful leads often come through acquaintances, not only close friends. For a founder, the same circle brings customers, partners and advice. You can be excellent and still be overlooked if no one knows what you are good at.

Skills get you in the room. Trust keeps you being invited back.

What it is made of

  • Trust: you do what you say, and people can count on you.
  • Reputation: people know what you are good at and can say it to someone else.
  • Reciprocity: you help without keeping score.
  • Reach: your circle is wider than the people sitting next to you.

Five weekly habits that build it

  • Give before you ask. Share a useful article, answer a question or make a helpful introduction.
  • Keep small promises. Reply when you said you would. Small reliability is noticed.
  • Thank people specifically. Say what they did and what it made possible.
  • Stay in touch when you need nothing. A short message to someone you have not spoken to in months.
  • Connect people. Introduce two people who should know each other, with a line on why.

Things that quietly spend it

  • Reaching out only when you need something.
  • Missing deadlines without saying so early.
  • Taking credit, or staying silent when others deserve it.

Try it this week

  • Send one specific thank-you.
  • Make one introduction.
  • Message one person you have not spoken to in a while, with no ask.
