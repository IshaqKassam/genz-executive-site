Answer first: how to write one page your manager will read

Most workplace writing fails for one reason: the reader has to dig for the point. Your manager is busy, reading on a phone between meetings. If the answer is in the fourth paragraph, it may never be read. The fix is simple and you can start today: put the answer first.

The four-line shape

For almost any update, request or recommendation, write four things in this order.

  • The answer or the ask. One sentence. “I recommend we move the launch to Thursday.”
  • Why. Two or three reasons, in order of importance.
  • What you need. A decision, a signature, feedback by a date.
  • What happens next. What you will do, and when.

Lead with the answer. Then earn the detail.

Before and after

Before: “Hi, I hope you are well. I was looking at the vendor quotes and there are a few things that came up, including pricing and timelines, so I wanted to share some thoughts...”

After: “I recommend we choose Vendor B. It is KES 40,000 cheaper and can deliver a week earlier. I need your approval by Wednesday to hold the date. I will send the purchase request as soon as you confirm.”

The second version can be read in ten seconds and acted on in one. (The names and numbers are an illustration.)

Five habits that make it work

  • Write the subject line last, and make it the answer: “Decision needed by Wednesday: vendor choice.”
  • Keep paragraphs to three lines. Use a short list when you have three or more parallel points.
  • Cut greetings that delay the point. Warmth belongs in the tone, not in a paragraph of throat-clearing.
  • Put numbers where they matter and say what they mean.
  • Read it once as your manager would: after ten seconds, do they know what to do?

Try it this week

  • Rewrite your next three emails in the four-line shape.
  • Ask one colleague if the point was clear in the first line.
  • Keep the version that worked as a template.
