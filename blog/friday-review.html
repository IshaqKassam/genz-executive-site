The Friday review: a ten-minute habit that compounds

Most people work hard all week and learn little from it, because they never stop to look. A Friday review fixes that. Ten minutes, the same time each week, and you start noticing patterns that would otherwise take years to see.

The five questions

  • What did I finish? List the outcomes, not the activity.
  • What went well, and why? Name the habit behind it so you can repeat it.
  • What did not go well? Facts, not blame. What would you do differently?
  • Who helped me, or who should I thank? Trust is built in small acknowledgements.
  • What is my one move next week? One, not ten.

Small reviews, repeated every week, beat big resolutions.

Why it compounds

A single review changes little. Fifty-two of them give you a record of what you learned, evidence for your next performance conversation, and a habit of acting on feedback. Managers notice people who can say precisely what they delivered and what they are improving.

Make it stick

  • Put it in your calendar, with an alert, at the same time every Friday.
  • Keep the notes in one place, such as a notebook or a single document.
  • Do it even in a bad week. Those are the weeks it helps most.
  • Share one lesson a month with your manager. It shows you take growth seriously.

Try it this week

  • Block ten minutes this Friday.
  • Answer the five questions in writing.
  • Pick one move for Monday and do it first.
